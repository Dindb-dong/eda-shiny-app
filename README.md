## Research Question

> During COVID-19, what moved card spending: case counts or government restrictions? A comparison of South Korea and Taiwan, 2020–2022.

The **Key findings** tab answers in three parts. Every number below is computed by the app from the data and asserted in `tests/check-app.R`; all are associations, not causal effects.

1. **Not case counts.** Both countries' case waves came in 2022 (97.8% of Korea's and 99.8% of Taiwan's 2020–2022 cases). In each country's three largest case months (Korea: March, April and August 2022; Taiwan: May, June and October 2022), year-over-year spending grew in 17 of 21 industry-months in Korea and 18 of 21 in Taiwan. Food and dining grew in 11 of 12 months of 2022 in Korea and in all 11 unmasked months in Taiwan (Spearman with cases +0.69 and +0.51). Korea's single largest month, March 2022, was mixed (4 of 7 industries grew), and Taiwan's May–June 2022 growth is partly a rebound from its 2021 alert. Locally the national pattern has an exception: in March 2020 the provinces at the centre of Korea's first outbreak fell most, with seven-industry spending down 33.3% year over year in Daegu (lowest of 16 provinces) and 23.2% in North Gyeongsang, against 15.2% nationwide and 13.4% in Seoul. The app has no province-level case or restriction data, so this is an association.
2. **Restrictions, at different times.** Korea was strict for 16 months (stringency 50 or more) across 2020–2021; its food and dining spending averaged −10.2% year over year in those months (r = −0.70 with stringency over 36 months), and 2020 spending fell 17.6% for apparel and 10.7% for food against 2019. Taiwan was strict for only 3 months, all in its 2021 Level 3 alert; from April to June 2021 apparel fell 38.2% (−3.2% over the same months of 2019), vehicles and air travel 32.6% (−3.2%) and health, education and recreation 25.7% (−4.1%), while source-defined Other rose 94.8%.
3. **An uneven hit that restrictions do not fully explain.** In 2020, with average stringency of only 24, Taiwan's health, education and recreation spending fell 35.3% and vehicles and air travel 14.5% while food and dining rose 20.4%. After removing a linear trend and one annual cycle, only Korean food and dining keeps a clear link to stringency (r −0.70 → −0.46).

Limits: 36 national monthly observations per country, shared trends and seasonality, nominal amounts, approximate industry matches, and a Taiwan source break in February 2021 that hides its alert from year-over-year comparisons.

The R Shiny dashboard joins each country's card spending to its own COVID-19 series. It covers seven spending categories and uses national COVID-19 measures for each selected card-spending area.

The spending filters use the 2019 index, year-over-year and month-over-month growth, a gap from the 2014–2019 calendar-month median, a 12-month log difference, or a combined first and 12-month log difference. Each transformation is computed before applying any period filter. The industry heatmaps clamp their colour scale at ±50 so that a few extreme months do not wash out the rest; exact values are in the CSV.

**Taiwan's February 2021 category shift.** Taiwan's NCCC category composition changes abruptly in February 2021: food falls from 38.7 to 18.7 billion TWD between January and February while general retail rises from 29.2 to 45.9 billion. The 12-month mean share of the seven-industry total moves from 14.9% to 9.3% for food and from 11.7% to 24.6% for general retail (February 2020 – January 2021 against February 2021 – January 2022), while apparel and home goods barely move. The step happens at once in every city and county (food falls in all 22; general retail rises in all but Matsu), and the January-to-February ratios of food and general retail move in opposite directions only in 2021 (×0.48 and ×1.57), whereas in every other year since 2015, including Lunar New Year shifts, they move together. NCCC and data.gov.tw document category definitions and earlier scope changes (SmartPay from July 2015; tax-payment platform data in e-commerce from September 2023) but publish no notice of a change in early 2021 (checked 25 November 2026). The pattern is what a change in merchant-to-industry mapping would produce and is unlike a consumption shift, so comparisons across that month are not shown; it is not confirmed that NCCC reclassified merchants. Because the shift is source-wide, the app sets every Taiwan change measure whose endpoints straddle it to missing in all industries and areas: year-over-year growth and the 12-month log difference for February 2021 – January 2022, month-over-month growth for February 2021, and the combined difference wherever an input is missing. The 2019 index and pre-COVID gap are level measures and cannot be repaired; their chart notes say so. Taiwan's strictest months (its May–July 2021 Level 3 alert) fall inside the masked window.

**Date coverage.** The card-only views use Jan 2014–Dec 2022, with Jan 2020 as the common cutoff. The relationship views pair Jan 2020–Dec 2022. JHU CSSE reports begin on 22 January 2020, so the first daily change is 23 January and January 2020 has no complete monthly case or death total; it is left missing rather than filled, while its stringency (complete from 1 January) is paired. A positive lead means the COVID-19 measure is from an earlier calendar month than the spending observation. No COVID-19 values are filled in for earlier years.

**Forecast and break models.** The fixed 2019 holdout trains through 2018 and compares seasonal naïve, additive Holt–Winters on log spending, and log-scale SARIMA (0,1,1)(0,1,1)[12] using MAE and RMSE in index points. A separate 2020–2022 projection refits through 2019. The segmented model estimates the January 2020 level break and change in monthly log slope with calendar-month effects and AR(1) errors. Both serve as descriptive historical references. Concurrent shocks and coverage changes can affect the apparent break.

### Nationwide card spending comparison

**Card spending comparison** integrates the standalone app supplied in [PR #5](https://github.com/Dindb-dong/eda-shiny-app/pull/5) into the existing navigation and report layout. The shared card/CPI window is January 2014–August 2023. Choose the total or any of eight categories, either country or both, and monthly values, a trailing 12-month average, or year-over-year growth. The default is a CPI-adjusted index with each country-category's 2014 monthly average equal to 100. Nominal indices and local currency amounts are also available; different currencies have separate panels.

The seven non-EC categories use offline spending. Taiwan e-commerce sums the online components of those seven categories; Korea supplies a separate EC series. Excluding Other also removes Taiwan's online Other but cannot do so for Korea's pre-aggregated EC series. The UI explains this asymmetry. Category labels and source coverage are approximate matches; this separate nationwide extract and its 2014 baseline differ from the joint analysis’s regional extract and 2019 baseline.

All indices, moving averages, and calendar-year comparisons are computed before clipping the displayed dates. Missing months remain missing rather than shifting the year-over-year lag or being counted as zero. The summary and CSV expose the selected monthly data, including original amounts, CPI, unit treatment and the Other-exclusion setting. Optional COVID shading and supplied reference restriction windows are chart annotations, not estimated treatment effects.

The original data files and their available provenance are in [`data/card_comparison/`](data/card_comparison/README.md). The contributor identifies Taiwan's card source as NCCC but has not yet supplied the Korean card and CPI source references.

### Capital-area card spending

- **Korea.** `capital` sums Seoul (`A`), Incheon (`D`), and Gyeonggi (`L`) under [Article 2 of the enforcement decree](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=277967). In ECOS `601Y002`, use monthly totals (`TOT`) and convert million won to won. `unallocated` is the source's Other (`Z`) series, and `non_capital` is the national total (`X`) minus `capital` and `unallocated`.
- **Taiwan.** `capital` sums Taipei (`TPE`), New Taipei (`NTP`), and Keelung (`KLC`). This city-level definition includes all of New Taipei, including Yingge, and excludes Taoyuan. `non_capital` sums the other 19 cities and counties. Amounts sum the published age bands for `FD`, `CT`, `EE`, `DP`, `LG`, `TR`, and `OT` from the [NCCC C02 API](https://www.nccc.com.tw/wps/wcm/connect/zh/home/openinformation/Open%20API). The 22 city and county totals reconcile to the published Taiwan series for every month and proxy, so `unallocated` is zero throughout. [Taiwan transport institute reference for the Taipei metropolitan area](https://www.iot.gov.tw/uploads/asset/data/66195d70367376304acd4ca6/%E4%BA%A4%E9%80%9A%E8%A1%8C%E5%8B%95%E6%9C%8D%E5%8B%99%28MaaS%29%E5%BE%8C%E7%BA%8C%E6%93%B4%E5%85%85%E8%88%87%E6%8E%A8%E5%BB%A3%E7%AD%96%E7%95%A5%E8%A6%8F%E5%8A%83.pdf)

The [industry crosswalk](data/industry_crosswalk.csv) lists the source codes for food and dining, apparel and accessories, health/education/recreation, general retail, lodging/furniture/appliances, vehicles/air travel, and source-defined Other. The first two retain the original definitions. Health/education/recreation and general retail are approximate. Lodging and vehicles cover narrower Korean proxies, and Other retains each source’s own definition. Interpret these three series within each country. E-commerce in the nationwide tab is a separate channel with different source coverage.

The crosswalk's channel coverage differs between countries: NCCC C02 industry amounts include online purchases, while ECOS records e-commerce (1200) as its own consumption type outside the seven industries. Online purchases were 28.1% of Taiwan's seven-industry card spending in 2019 and 39.0% in 2022 (food: 11.1% and 51.9%), using the nationwide extract's online split. Cross-country differences in growth, especially in food and general retail after 2020, can therefore partly reflect the move to online spending in Taiwan rather than a difference in consumption.

The added monthly amounts were retrieved from ECOS and NCCC C02 on 23 November 2026. For every added Taiwan series, the sum of all 22 city and county amounts matches the published national figure. For 2019–2022, both countries’ added national totals also match the corresponding source codes in the repository’s national extracts. The regions describe where card spending is recorded. Confirmed cases and Korea's distancing levels are available for the same two zones (see the files above); deaths and the Stringency Index remain national.

Korean regions follow where the payment is recorded, not where the cardholder lives, and this matters most for e-commerce. In ECOS `601Y002` (retrieved from the ECOS Open API on 26 November 2026), the capital area recorded 98.4% of national e-commerce (1200) card spending in 2019 and 99.4% in 2022, Seoul alone 80.1% and 80.4%. E-commerce was 28.6% and 36.8% of Seoul's card spending but 0.5% and 0.2% in the 13 non-capital provinces. Over the same years, recorded e-commerce rose 74.9% in the capital area and fell 41.5% elsewhere. The regional analysis therefore leaves Korean e-commerce out of the seven industries and has no e-commerce category; Taiwan's online spending cannot be separated by region either. Even without e-commerce the capital area records 66.6% (2019) and 68.0% (2022) of Korean card spending, so merchant location also raises the capital share of the other industries.

Both regional files include `covid_period` and use January 2020 as the cutoff for both countries. `pre_covid` covers January 2014 through December 2019, and `since_covid_onset` covers January 2020 through December 2022.

## Analysis Approach

1. **Compare the pandemic and spending series at a common frequency.** For the joint analysis, sum daily reported cases and deaths by month, average the daily Stringency Index, and align the results with spending by country and calendar month.
2. **Compare relative spending changes.** Use each country and category's own 2019 average as the index baseline and use earlier card data to calculate year-over-year growth. Use the industry crosswalk to check comparability before interpreting cross-country differences.
3. **Check how much the interpretation depends on the comparison.** Compare patterns with cases, deaths, and stringency within each country. Show how the association changes with the spending measure, 0–3 month leads, and removal of trend and seasonality. Taiwan's change measures that straddle its February 2021 category shift are masked rather than interpreted.

The joint window covers up to 36 months per country (24 for Taiwan's year-over-year growth after masking). Seasonality, shared trends, and differences in card coverage can all shape the apparent relationships. The synthesis will describe exploratory associations with those limits in view.
